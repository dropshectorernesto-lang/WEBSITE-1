GROOMING BASICS

HOW OFTEN SHOULD YOU ACTUALLY BATHE YOUR DOG?

Grüm explains how to find the right bath rhythm for your dog's coat type, activity level, and skin sensitivity — and why "once a month" isn't a one-size-fits-all rule.

Most dogs do well with a bath every four to six weeks, but the right schedule depends on coat type, skin sensitivity, activity level, and how much time they spend outside.

At Grüm, short-coated dogs may need less frequent baths, while curly, long, or double-coated dogs often need more regular professional care to prevent buildup and matting.

If your dog smells, feels greasy, scratches more than usual, or leaves extra dirt on bedding, Grüm can help choose a gentler rhythm before skin gets irritated.

COAT CARE

BRUSHING BETWEEN VISITS: A 5-MINUTE ROUTINE THAT WORKS

Grüm shares the simple brushing routine we recommend to clients to prevent mats, reduce shedding, and make every grooming appointment faster and more comfortable.

A short brushing routine a few times a week can make a huge difference, especially around the ears, chest, tail, belly, and legs where mats start fastest.

Grüm recommends slow passes, small sections, and stopping if your dog gets uncomfortable. The goal is consistency, not a wrestling match.

Regular brushing helps keep Grüm appointments calmer because the coat is easier to wash, dry, and style. It also lets you spot skin changes before they become bigger problems.

NAIL & PAW CARE

SIGNS YOUR DOG'S NAILS ARE OVERDUE FOR A TRIM

Grüm breaks down the signs your dog's nails are overdue, from clicking on hard floors to curling tips and changes in how your dog walks.

If you hear clicking on hard floors, see nails touching the ground while your dog stands, or notice curling tips, it is probably time for a trim.

Grüm checks nail length as part of comfortable dog grooming because long nails can change posture and make walking less comfortable.

For nervous pets, shorter and more frequent Grüm nail appointments can be easier than waiting until the nails are very long.

FIRST VISIT

WHAT TO EXPECT AT YOUR DOG'S FIRST GRÜM APPOINTMENT

Nervous about your pet's first professional groom? Here's exactly how a Grüm session runs, start to finish — from check-in to the one-on-one attention that makes the whole visit lower-stress.

The first Grüm visit starts with a quick check-in so we can learn about your dog's coat, comfort level, health notes, and the service you want.

From there, Grüm moves at your pet's pace. A one-on-one appointment keeps things calmer because your dog is not waiting in a crowded space.

After the groom, Grüm will let you know how the coat did, what maintenance helps at home, and when the next appointment should be.
